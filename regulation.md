# Regulating the Four Basic Drives

Practical notes on managing the four primitive drives (food, sleep, sex, and self-preservation). The core idea, drawn from Swami Rama’s *The Art of Joyful Living* and related teachings, is that these drives are basically the same in humans and animals. The human capacity is the ability to understand, arrange, and regulate them instead of being run by them.

When the drives are unregulated they feed the common streams of negative emotion (desire, anger, pride, attachment, greed, jealousy, egotism). When they are regulated, the same energy becomes usable.

## Four Jobs of the Mind

These are four functions of one inner instrument, not four separate minds. Swami Rama’s image is a wheel: the rim is the body acting in the world, the four functions are the spokes, and the still center is the Self.

| Job | What it does | When it runs the show |
|-----|--------------|-----------------------|
| Sensing & processing | Takes in sense data, questions, doubts, sends responses out | Chatter, second-guessing, following old habits |
| Memory & habit | Stores impressions, memories, emotional coloring | Replaying old films so you react from the past |
| Sense of self | Stamps “this is me / this is mine”; useful identity | Builds a shell, refuses the still center, wants to be the owner |
| Clear judgment | Discriminates, decides, tells helpful from harmful | Should have the last word |

Intended order: sensing gathers data → memory supplies relevant history → sense of self personalizes it → clear judgment decides. When habits or the sense of self override clear judgment, you know what you should do and do the opposite.

The practical goal is coordination so the four jobs work together instead of fighting.

## Four Basic Drives

All negative emotional streams trace back to one of these four. There is no fifth.

| Drive | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Food | Body-first. Diet that agitates the nervous system feeds emotional chaos. |
| Sleep | Necessity of body and mind. Quality of rest matters more than hours. True rest is higher pleasure than food or sex. |
| Sex | Mind-first. Neither taboo nor obsessive importance. Frustration often leaks into other drives. |
| Self-preservation | Strongest of the four. Fear, clinging, and defensiveness often trace here. “Fear invites danger.” |

They are linked: mess one up and the others tilt. Self-preservation is the strongest; sex is the weakest in the sense that one can live without it.

Not every positive impulse comes from these drives. The drive to know or love what is true has a different source.

## Practical Training Plan

Goal: observe which drive is active, then adjust one variable so the energy can be used instead of flooding the system.

### Daily observation
When you notice anger, greed, restlessness, fear, or compulsive behavior, pause and scan the four drives:
- Which one is active right now?
- Is it timing, quantity, quality, or attitude that is off?

### One-variable adjustment
Pick only one change and hold it for a week. Examples:
- Food: fixed meal times, or remove one agitating item.
- Sleep: same bedtime, or a short deliberate rest (calm breath, mind free of content) before bed.
- Sex: notice the urge without acting on impulse; keep it under conscious control.
- Self-preservation: when fear arises, examine whether the threat is real or a false identity being defended.

### Weekly review
At the end of the week, note:
- Which drive leaked most often?
- Did the single adjustment reduce the flood?
- What one change for the next week?

### Supporting practice
Use the four jobs of the mind as a check: is clear judgment still having the last word, or have habit and the sense of self taken over? Bring the decision back to clear judgment.

### Compact daily reminder
Peaceful going to sleep.  
Cheerful while eating.  
Fully under control with sex.  
Examine fear instead of feeding it.
